# Phase 314 - Ask Why Before Opening

Every hosting company that runs this panel is the processor of its customers' personal data, and the people it hosts for
have a right to expect that nobody looks at their files, their mail or their visitors without a good reason. Going through
what an administrator could actually reach turned up several doors with no lock: signing in as a customer needed no reason,
a backup could be browsed and downloaded freely, a site's web server log, which names every visitor, could be downloaded, and
a password the administrator chose or saw was a way in that skipped even the sign-in record.

## What changed

Each of those now stops and asks why. The reasons are the customer asked for help, a suspected breach of the terms, a
legal or authority request, or a security incident, each with a note that has to mean something to a later reader. A
reason is good for half an hour, for one account, and everything done under it is recorded with it. The server logs now hide
visitors' addresses and what they asked for, the way the mail log already did, and the record of who looked at what is kept
for two years and then removed.

A new guide sets out what an administrator can and cannot see, and what a hosting company still has to do for itself:
agreements with its customers, telling people, and reporting a breach in time.
